## Origin story

Some of the first things I ever wrote were programs, in BASIC, on a TRS-80. One of them was a text adventure. The thrill was specific: type the right words and the machine does the thing. I've been chasing a version of that ever since.

Then human language won, for a while. Latin, French, Spanish, Italian. Latin is where *movēre* comes from: to move, the root of emotion and motivation, and eventually the name of the method this studio was built on. A dead language turned out to be one of the most useful things I studied.

## Disney

During college I spent summers in the Magic Kingdom, working the Davy Crockett Explorer Canoes. No motor, no track. The guests paddled, and from the bow I delivered scripted, corny jokes while a colleague steered from the stern; we rotated through the day. After college, Disney was one of the first accounts I worked on at my first agency job, and the writing turned professional: copy for exhibits, rides and restaurants across the parks and hotels, and the full-color international newsletter for the Disney Vacation Club, which I wrote and edited. It's where I learned that the voice has to hold from a ride sign to a newsletter to a menu to a canoe.

## Hard Rock

Hard Rock Cafe International was my other key account at that agency, until they hired me away. For several years I ran a department at the international headquarters, and the work was the whole brand at once: I produced and launched the first Hard Rock Cafe International website; I conceived, produced, launched and managed the Hard Rock Mystery Tour, a global sweepstakes promotion whose materials and music video featured in every restaurant, and whose video won a Telly Award; and I edited, designed and directed *The Hard Rocker*, the full-color international newsletter, then launched a smaller magazine, *Rock Hard News*, alongside it. In-store traffic rose 15% year over year during the Mystery Tour. The newsletters went out in 30 languages to more than 65 countries, which is where I learned that a brand's voice has to survive translation or it isn't done right.

## The agency years

Fantasy Interactive, Organic, and Razorfish, and launch work for Google, Intel, Sony, Nickelodeon and others. Google's Project Re:Brief rebuilt four classic campaigns for the modern web and won a Cannes Cyber Lion. The Intel work drove a 25% lift in sales and online conversions. The Nickelodeon Kids' Choice Awards app lifted engagement 20% year over year. Every one of those is a card in [the work](/work/). It's work I led and produced.

## Accenture Interactive: the pitch rooms

The win rate went from 54% to 88% across more than 20 enterprise pitches, with over $1B in attributed revenue. Every pitch ran like a startup brand: a three-hour session with the senior stakeholders in the room, then a fully formed identity, lexicon and narrative within weeks, and one unarguable metric at the end. Win or lose.

Same problem as a founder's, at a different scale: a room that has to believe a story the person who knows it best won't be there to tell. That three-hour session is where the Rumble started. It's the second step of every engagement now, right after Unlock.

## CRC: the studio

ChrisRubinCreativ is the house. For founder-led companies, the studio extracts the story the founder carries around in their head and builds it into the identity, the positioning and the words the company actually uses: Apto Solutions (+41% revenue), Remark Growth Marketing (+36% revenue), Tria Beauty (+63% website revenue), Ledger (+20% monthly sales through its industry's worst downturn), BetterCloud (25% market share regained, and a move from Gartner Visionary to Gartner Leader). The [case studies](/work/) carry the detail.

<H2 id="method">The method, three generations</H2>

The method has been rebuilt twice, each time to travel further from me, maturing in depth and resolution.

1. **Movere** (CRC): Brand Clarity, to know thyself. Brand Positioning, to know your audience. Brand Resonance, to move people. Brand Extension, to keep moving them. Four steps, run by hand, one company at a time.
1. **The Storyline Method**: Anchor, Insight, Shift, Unification, Realization. The shape a story has to take before a team can tell it faithfully, with or without the founder.
1. **The Narrative Operating System** (BrandMultiplier): Unlock, Rumble, Architect, Install, Tune. Extraction, then installation into how the whole company sells, then software that keeps it current. This is the current practice, and it's what [BrandMultiplier](https://www.brandmultiplier.ai) does.
